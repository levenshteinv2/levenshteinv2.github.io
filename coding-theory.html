Levenshtein Distance in Coding Theory and Synchronization Codes

Coding theory provides one of the original and most fundamental settings for Levenshtein distance: designing codewords that remain distinguishable when a communication or storage channel introduces deletions, insertions, substitutions, or other synchronization errors. Unlike substitution-only channels governed naturally by Hamming distance, insertion and deletion channels can shift every subsequent symbol and therefore require a different combinatorial geometry, decoding theory, and redundancy analysis. This section develops that connection from minimum edit distance and correction guarantees to modern insertion-deletion codes, synchronization methods, and the classical Varshamov-Tenengolts and Levenshtein constructions that established the foundations of indel-correcting codes.

Landau–Vishkin 1988: Fast String Matching with k Differences, Approximate Pattern Matching, and Error-Bounded Search

Landau–Vishkin 1988: Fast String Matching with k Differences, Approximate Pattern Matching, and Error-Bounded Search

Gad M. Landau and Uzi Vishkin's 1988 paper Fast String Matching with k Differences is a foundational work in approximate pattern matching under edit distance.

It is also easy to misdescribe.

A common modern summary says:

Landau–Vishkin
=
O(nk) edit-distance matching
using diagonals and LCE queries.

That statement refers to an important Landau–Vishkin algorithmic lineage, but it is not an exact description of the particular 1988 Journal of Computer and System Sciences paper analyzed on this page.

The published 1988 paper states the following serial complexities for finding every approximate occurrence of a pattern of length m in a text of length n with at most k differences:

fixed-size alphabet:

O(
 m + n k²
)

general input:

O(
 m log m + n k²
)

space:

O(m).

The later Landau–Vishkin paper Fast Parallel and Serial Approximate String Matching, published in 1989 from a different line of their work, gives the better:

O(nk)

serial bound for a fixed alphabet.

Keeping those results separate is essential for a historically accurate account.

The 1988 paper's importance does not rest solely on one asymptotic expression. It belongs to the period in which approximate matching was being transformed from a full:

m × n

dynamic-programming computation into a problem organized around:

  • a small error parameter k;
  • edit-matrix diagonals;
  • furthest progress after a bounded number of differences;
  • long exact matching regions between errors;
  • string preprocessing;
  • and data structures capable of skipping those exact regions efficiently.

The paper therefore occupies an important position between Ukkonen's distance-sensitive edit-distance algorithms and later high-performance approximate pattern-matching algorithms.

This article analyzes the exact problem Landau and Vishkin solve, the meaning of a difference, the modified dynamic-programming matrix for approximate occurrences, the diagonal/frontier viewpoint behind the algorithmic family, the role of exact extensions between errors, the distinction between k mismatches and k differences, the published 1988 complexity bounds, the relationship to the 1985 FOCS precursor, and the later improvement to O(nk).
